The change

From a bird to a person.

The old mark was an owl shape. This one is a portrait, and the difference is the clothing: a collar gives him shoulders, and shoulders give him a job. The tie is the brand gold already used for the beak, so the accent runs on a single vertical axis — beak, knot, blade.

Construction

Six parts, and what each is doing.

Nothing here is decoration. Every element carries either recognition or meaning, which is what lets the mark survive being shrunk to a favicon.

Tufts
Short and blunt. Long tufts read as a devil or a cat at small sizes. These sit just proud of the head and stop.
Eyes
The recognisable part. Brand blue at #019FFF, wide and forward. They are the first thing that survives when everything else goes.
Beak
Gold, on the centre line. A small diamond, not a hook — he reads as attentive rather than predatory.
Collar
White, symmetrical, two flaps. The flaps tuck behind the head so the join is never visible. This is what turns the shape into a portrait.
Tie
Brand gold, knotted. The knot emerges from under the chin; the blade tapers to a point above the lower edge, never touching it.
Shoulders
Cropped by the frame. The torso runs off the bottom of the artboard, which is what makes it a portrait rather than a full figure.
Reproduction

He has to work at both ends.

At 64 px and below, use the flat variant — it drops the soft shading, the eye highlights and the tie fold, which turn to mud at small sizes. Below 24 px, use the avatar crop rather than the full portrait.

Lockups

With the name beside him, and above him.

Clear space around the lockup is one head-width on every side. The mark never appears inside a box, a badge or a circle except as an app icon or an avatar, where the crop is the frame.

Handling

Four things to hold, four to avoid.

Do

Give him room

One head-width of clear space on every side. He is a portrait, and portraits need a margin to read as one.

Don't

Recolour the tie

The gold is the accent and it is fixed. A blue tie loses the only warm note on the mark; a red one leaves the palette entirely.

Do

Drop to flat below 64 px

The shading and highlights are there for the hero. Small sizes get the flat variant, which is the same geometry with the soft passes removed.

Don't

Outline him

No keyline, no drop shadow, no stroke around the silhouette. If he will not hold on a ground, change the ground or use the one-colour variant.

Do

Use the avatar crop for icons

App icon, favicon, profile picture, email signature. The crop keeps the head, the collar and the top of the knot — enough to identify him in a tab.

Don't

Stretch or rotate

Scale proportionally and keep him upright. He is a professional, not a mascot on a skateboard.

Do

Let him carry a line

Beside a quotation, in a message thread, next to a section heading. He works hardest as a speaker, not as wallpaper.

Don't

Put him on every screen

A character that appears everywhere stops meaning anything. He earns a place where a person would speak.
