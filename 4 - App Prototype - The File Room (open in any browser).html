What this prototype is claiming

The idea

The file never closes

Most tax portals are a drop box that wakes up in February. This one is built around the firm's actual position — the file is open in June and October too, and the app is where that is visible.

The discipline

Never “send everything”

Every screen names the specific missing thing. A checklist that says 1099-MISC from Keystone gets answered; one that says upload your documents does not.

The constraint

It does not compute tax

No calculation engine, no advice generated without a human. The app moves documents, signatures, answers and money. The return is prepared in the firm's software by a person.

The hard part

Signing is the regulated bit

Remote signing of Form 8879 needs knowledge-based identity verification, a three-attempt limit, a fallback to wet signature, and a tamper-proof three-year evidence record. That is the piece to buy, not build.

The obligation

Building it means owning it

A firm that runs its own portal is a financial institution under the FTC Safeguards Rule: a named Qualified Individual, written risk assessment, MFA, encryption, annual penetration testing, and a 30-day breach clock.

The tone

Uncle Pat, quietly

The character belongs in the app the way a good adviser belongs in a room — a line at the right moment, not a mascot on every screen. He explains; he never sells.
